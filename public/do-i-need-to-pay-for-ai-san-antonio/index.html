The test

When is paying someone actually the right call?

Pay someone when three things are true at the same time: the task repeats at least weekly, the process has settled so you are not automating a draft, and the yearly cost of doing it by hand is more than the one time build price. If a job comes up twice a month, do it by hand. If you changed the steps in the last 60 days, wait until they stop moving. If a $250 build will not save you more than $250 of work in a year, it is not the thing to fix first.

Test one

It repeats about weekly

At least four times a month, every month. Under that, the automation spends more of your attention than the task does. A quarterly report is not an automation problem, it is a calendar reminder.

Test two

The process has settled

You have not changed the steps in about 60 days. Automating a process you are still redesigning means paying to build it twice. Write the steps down by hand first and run them for a month.

Test three

The outcome beats the build

What the task costs you over a year has to exceed the one time price. Twenty minutes a week is about 17 hours a year, which clears a $250 build for most owners. Twenty minutes a month does not.
